<h1>Human-Robot Interaction Transcripts: Reminiscence Therapy Studies</h1>

<p>This repository contains the dialogue transcripts for two research studies (Study C and Study D) exploring <strong>User Engagement</strong> during socially assistive robotics-based <strong>Reminiscence Therapy (RT)</strong>.</p>

<p>While the broader research involves multimodal data (head pose, gaze, and voice activation), this repository specifically hosts the <strong>verbal interaction data</strong> used to provide qualitative social context and lexical metrics.</p>

<div class="folder-box">
    <div class="badge">Folder 1</div>
    <h2>Study C: Assessing User Engagement</h2>
    <p><em>Focus: Identifying and correlating multimodal communication cues.</em></p>
    <ul>
        <li><strong>Participants:</strong> 16 individuals.</li>
        <li><strong>Task:</strong> Foundational Reminiscence Therapy dialogue.</li>
        <li><strong>Data Purpose:</strong> Used to validate physical markers of engagement against qualitative linguistic behaviors.</li>
        <li><strong>Key Finding:</strong> Integrated lexical and behavioral cues correlated more strongly with engagement than unimodal analysis in 68% of cases.</li>
    </ul>
</div>

<div class="folder-box">
    <div class="badge">Folder 2</div>
    <h2>Study D: Role of Verbal Feedback</h2>
    <p><em>Focus: Comparing static responses vs. LLM-generated (GPT-3.5) dynamic feedback.</em></p>
    <ul>
        <li><strong>Participants:</strong> 15 healthy participants.</li>
        <li><strong>Task:</strong> Reflection on personal photographs with the Pepper robot.</li>
        <li><strong>Conditions:</strong> <code>Minimal Feedback</code> (Generic) vs. <code>Detailed Feedback</code> (LLM-generated).</li>
        <li><strong>Key Finding:</strong> High-quality semantic feedback fosters "social wonder" and genuine social presence, whereas minimal feedback triggers compensatory verbal effort from the user.</li>
    </ul>
</div>

<h2>Data Organization</h2>
<ul>
    <li>Files are organized by participant ID within each study folder.</li>
    <li>Transcripts focus purely on the verbal exchange between the human participant and the robotic agent.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Research Context</h2>
<p>These transcripts support the analysis of how conversational strategies shape embodied interaction. By triangulating this text with physical metrics (captured separately), the research establishes that physical-affective signals are sensitive indicators of interaction quality, but semantic richness is essential for sustaining meaningful engagement.</p>

<div class="footer">
    <p>Research conducted in the field of Socially Assistive Robotics (SAR) and Human-Computer Interaction (HCI).</p>
